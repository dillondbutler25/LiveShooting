4. Before you shoot

  • Prop the phone up about waist high, 10–15 feet away, standing up tall (portrait).
  • Your whole body plus your arms overhead must fit in the frame. Check the screen before you start.
  • Turn the volume up. If you hear nothing, flip the ringer switch on.
  • Shoot form shots first, 5–10 feet out. Then move back.
  • After each shot, bring the ball back down to your waist to reset.

What it grades and where the standards come from

Every cue maps to a fundamental taught by coaches and backed by shooting research. Numbers marked "starting point" are first guesses that should be tuned after real testing.

  • Legs: knee bend at the bottom of the dip. Good shooters bend to about 105–115° (180° = straight). Cabarkapa et al. 2021 and 2023.
  • Set point: stop the ball around the forehead, in front of the face and over the shooting eye, not on top of the head or behind it. Wissel; Engelland's work with Kawhi Leonard; jr.nba.com.
  • Elbow: forearm close to straight up and down. The most upright forearm separated makes from misses in Cabarkapa 2021.
  • Release: elbow nearly straight (about 160–168° at release in Cabarkapa 2023) and the arm going up, not out.
  • Rhythm: release around the top of the jump. Satern 1993; Podmenik 2021 found elite shooters release 0–0.12 s after the peak on short and mid shots.
  • Guide hand: stays on the side of the ball and adds no push. Breakthrough Basketball; Wissel.
  • Follow-through: hold it until the ball reaches the rim, wrist snapped, fingers to the rim. Wissel; jr.nba.com.
  • Balance: feet about shoulder width, land close to where you took off. Wissel; Breakthrough Basketball.

Full source list in the research page. The camera can't see the ball, so arc, spin and makes aren't graded yet.
